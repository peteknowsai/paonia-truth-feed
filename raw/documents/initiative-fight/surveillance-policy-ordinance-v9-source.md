# Citizen Initiative Petition — v9 (Surveillance Policy Ordinance)

**Drafted 2026-08-01.** Builds on v7's architecture, NOT v8's. The June 9 rejection proved
v8's narrowing strategy backwards: replacing the Board-authority limitation with performance
verbs ("conduct, cause, or permit") handed the Clerk operational language to quote back as
administrative. v9 restores v7's authorization-layer operative section and adds the
"legislative-character armor" now shared with the Data Center Policy Ordinance:

1. **New WHEREAS** placing the measure in the established category of contract-condition
   laws (TABOR — itself citizen-initiated — bidding ordinances, debt limits).
2. **Authorization-layer verb discipline** in § 2(b), with an express sentence ceding
   negotiation/execution/administration.
3. **New § 6 (Prospective Operation; Relation to Administrative Functions)** — prospective
   only, no impairment of existing contracts (this kills the April 27 letter's Verkada
   "existing contractual obligation" theory outright), no administrative direction, rule of
   authorization.

Strategy note: submit AFTER the Data Center Policy Ordinance has been submitted and acted
on. If the data center petition is approved, this goes in with a cover letter noting the
town certified identical operative language as legislative. If rejected, this waits for the
side-by-side exhibit and the trustee-verbatim-introduction play.

## SUMMARY OF PROPOSED INITIATIVE ORDINANCE (warning-page block) ⟨REVISED⟩

Shall the Town of Paonia adopt an ordinance establishing a civic right of residents and
visitors to be free from continuous electronic surveillance by the Town in public spaces;
declaring it to be the public policy of the Town that the Town shall not authorize, fund, or
contract for such surveillance; defining "continuous electronic surveillance" and "public
spaces"; providing narrow exceptions for body-worn law-enforcement cameras during active
enforcement encounters, warrant-authorized cameras for specific criminal investigations of
limited duration, cameras used solely to record open public meetings, and cameras monitoring
unmanned critical infrastructure facilities not accessible to the general public; prohibiting
the Town Board from delegating to any person or entity the authority to conduct such
surveillance on the Town's behalf; declaring that the ordinance operates prospectively only,
does not impair any existing contract, and neither directs nor withdraws any administrative
function of the Town; declaring the ordinance to be of permanent and general character; and
providing for severability and an effective date upon certification of the vote?

## TEXT OF PROPOSED INITIATIVE

**ORDINANCE NO. 2026-__**

**AN INITIATED ORDINANCE OF THE PEOPLE OF THE TOWN OF PAONIA, COLORADO, ESTABLISHING THE
CIVIC RIGHT TO ANONYMITY IN PUBLIC SPACES, DECLARING THE PUBLIC POLICY OF THE TOWN
CONCERNING CONTINUOUS ELECTRONIC SURVEILLANCE, AND LIMITING THE LEGISLATIVE AUTHORITY OF THE
TOWN BOARD TO AUTHORIZE OR FUND SUCH SURVEILLANCE.**

### Section 1. Findings and Declaration of Public Policy.

**WHEREAS**, the Town of Paonia is a statutory municipality organized under C.R.S.
§ 31-1-203, and the registered electors of the Town have reserved to themselves the power of
initiative under Article V, Section 1(9) of the Colorado Constitution and C.R.S. § 31-11-104;

**WHEREAS**, the Town's public streets, sidewalks, parks, plazas, and other public
rights-of-way are held in trust for the use and benefit of all residents and visitors;

**WHEREAS**, the civic character of Paonia — as a community of approximately fifteen hundred
residents historically marked by practical anonymity, neighborliness, and trust between
residents and their local government — depends in part on the absence of pervasive
electronic monitoring of public life;

**WHEREAS**, the Colorado Constitution at Article II, Section 7 provides that "the people
shall be secure in their persons, papers, homes and effects, from unreasonable searches and
seizures," and at Article II, Section 10 protects the rights of "free speech" and "the
press," and continuous electronic surveillance of public spaces by the Town government
implicates these rights by chilling the exercise of lawful assembly, speech, and civic
participation;

**WHEREAS**, the Colorado General Assembly has legislated on government surveillance
technology, including Senate Bill 22-113 (regulating the use of facial recognition
technology by government agencies) and House Bill 24-1130 (governing biometric identifier
collection, sale, and retention), thereby treating government surveillance as a subject
appropriate to legislative action;

**WHEREAS** ⟨NEW⟩, ordinances establishing general and prospective conditions upon the
exercise of a municipality's contracting and spending powers — competitive-bidding and
purchasing ordinances, debt limitations, and the elector-approval requirements of Article X,
Section 20 of the Colorado Constitution, itself adopted by citizen initiative — are an
established and quintessentially legislative category of law: such measures execute no
contract, manage no property, and direct no employee, but define in advance the conditions
under which such executive acts may be authorized;

**WHEREAS**, this Ordinance does not concern the management or operation of any Town
property or asset; it concerns the relationship between the Town government and the people
who reside in, work in, and visit the Town of Paonia;

**WHEREAS**, the question whether a municipal government shall conduct continuous electronic
surveillance of its residents and visitors in public spaces is a question of fundamental
public policy of permanent and general character, properly the subject of municipal
legislation under the people's reserved initiative power, and not a question of
administrative implementation;

**WHEREAS**, the people of the Town of Paonia are exercising the Town's police power under
C.R.S. §§ 31-15-101 and 31-15-401 to protect the health, safety, welfare, and civic liberty
of the Town's residents and visitors;

**NOW, THEREFORE, the people of the Town of Paonia HEREBY DECLARE** it to be the public
policy of the Town of Paonia that continuous electronic surveillance of persons in public
spaces by the Town government is incompatible with the civic character of Paonia and with
the rights of the Town's residents and visitors, and **HEREBY ORDAIN** the following:

### Section 2. Civic Right to Anonymity in Public Spaces; Limitation on Town Authority. ⟨REVISED⟩

(a) **Right Established.** The residents and visitors of the Town of Paonia have a civic
right, enforceable against the Town and its agents, to be free from continuous electronic
surveillance by the Town in public spaces. This right is a permanent and general policy of
the Town of Paonia within the meaning of Article V, Section 1(9) of the Colorado
Constitution.

(b) **Limitation on Town Board Authority.** It shall be unlawful for the Town of Paonia, or
the Town Board acting on its behalf, to adopt any ordinance or resolution, or to approve or
authorize any contract, license, or budget appropriation, that funds, authorizes, or
otherwise enables continuous electronic surveillance of persons in public spaces. This
subsection operates solely upon the Town Board's acts of adoption, approval, and
authorization; it directs no act of contract negotiation, execution, performance, or
administration.

### Section 3. Definitions.

For purposes of this Ordinance:

(a) **"Continuous electronic surveillance"** means the persistent or ongoing capture,
recording, transmission, storage, or analysis of visual images, biometric identifiers, or
location data of persons in public spaces by means of electronic equipment, whether operated
in real time or by recording, and whether analog or digital. The term includes systems that
continuously monitor a defined area; it does not include the temporary or incidental capture
of images attendant to an individual law-enforcement encounter, an open public meeting, or
other event-specific activity authorized under Section 4.

(b) **"Public spaces"** means any real property within the Town of Paonia that is owned,
leased, or controlled by the Town and is open to or used by members of the general public,
including but not limited to streets, sidewalks, rights-of-way, parks, plazas, public
buildings during hours of public access, and the doorways and entrances thereof.

(c) **"The Town"** means the Town of Paonia, Colorado, including the Town Board, all Town
departments, all elected and appointed officials, all employees, all agents, and all
contractors acting on behalf of the Town.

(d) **"Authorize"** means to enable by ordinance, resolution, contract, budget
appropriation, license, permit, formal directive, or any other exercise of Town legislative,
executive, or contracting authority.

### Section 4. Exceptions.

The right and prohibition established in Section 2 do not apply to the following, which are
not "continuous electronic surveillance" within the meaning of this Ordinance:

(a) **Body-worn cameras** worn by law enforcement officers and activated only during the
course of an active enforcement encounter, governed by separate Town policy and applicable
state law;

(b) **Warrant-authorized cameras** used temporarily for a specific criminal investigation
pursuant to a court order, which shall be removed upon expiration of the order;

(c) **Public-meeting cameras** used solely to record or live-stream open public meetings of
the Town for the purpose of transparency, provided such cameras are not used for any purpose
other than recording the meeting itself;

(d) **Critical infrastructure cameras** directed solely at unmanned Town infrastructure
facilities (such as water treatment, electrical, or pump-house facilities) that are not
accessible to the general public, provided such cameras do not capture images of public
streets, sidewalks, parks, or other public spaces accessible to the public.

These exceptions are an integral part of the substantive policy declared in Section 2 and
define the scope of the civic right established therein.

### Section 5. No Delegation.

The Town Board may not authorize, contract with, fund, license, permit, or otherwise enable
any person or entity — public or private — to conduct continuous electronic surveillance of
persons in public spaces on the Town's behalf or under the Town's authority. This Ordinance
applies with equal force to direct Town operation of surveillance equipment and to
third-party operation authorized, funded, or facilitated by the Town.

### Section 6. Prospective Operation; Relation to Administrative Functions. ⟨NEW⟩

(a) **Prospective Only.** This Ordinance operates prospectively only. It does not rescind,
modify, or impair any contract or agreement in existence on its effective date; it does not
direct the performance or administration of any existing contract; and it does not affect
any vested right.

(b) **No Administrative Direction.** Nothing in this Ordinance directs, manages, or
administers any specific Town facility, operation, contract, transaction, or personnel
decision; approves or disapproves any pending application or agreement; or withdraws from
the Town's administrative staff any function committed to them. The performance and
administration of any contract or activity consistent with this Ordinance remain executive
functions of the Town.

(c) **Rule of Authorization.** This Ordinance operates solely upon the authorization layer
of Town government: it defines the conditions governing the Town Board's adoption or
approval of the acts enumerated in Section 2(b). It is a law about what may be authorized,
not a directive about how anything shall be done.

### Section 7. Permanent and General Character.

This Ordinance is permanent in operation. It contains no sunset provision. It applies to all
departments, officers, employees, agents, and contractors of the Town of Paonia, in all
geographic areas subject to the Town's jurisdiction, at all times. It declares a public
policy of permanent and general character within the meaning of Article V, Section 1(9) of
the Colorado Constitution.

### Section 8. Liberal Construction.

This Ordinance shall be liberally construed to effectuate its purpose of protecting the
civic right to anonymity in public spaces established in Section 2.

### Section 9. Severability.

If any provision of this Ordinance, or its application to any person or circumstance, is
held invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, the remaining provisions and applications
shall continue in full force and effect.

### Section 10. Effective Date.

This Ordinance takes effect upon certification of the vote.
