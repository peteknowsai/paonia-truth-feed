## SUMMARY OF PROPOSED INITIATIVE ORDINANCE (warning-page block)

Shall the Town of Paonia adopt an ordinance establishing the public policy of the Town concerning
data center facilities; reserving to the registered electors of the Town the decision whether to
commit the Town's municipal water, wastewater, infrastructure, real property, or fiscal resources
to any data center facility; declaring that the Town shall not authorize, fund, contract for, or
otherwise enable such facilities absent approval by the electors at a regular or special municipal
election; defining "data center facility" and related terms; providing narrow exceptions for the
Town's own municipal information systems, public safety communications equipment, local
broadband and telecommunications distribution equipment, and on-premises computing incidental
to the operations of local businesses and institutions; prohibiting the Town Board from delegating
to or accomplishing through any person or entity what this ordinance prohibits directly; declaring
the ordinance to be of permanent and general character; and providing for severability and an
effective date upon certification of the vote?

## TEXT OF PROPOSED INITIATIVE

**ORDINANCE NO. 2026-__**

**AN INITIATED ORDINANCE OF THE PEOPLE OF THE TOWN OF PAONIA, COLORADO, ESTABLISHING THE
PUBLIC POLICY OF THE TOWN CONCERNING DATA CENTER FACILITIES, RESERVING TO THE REGISTERED
ELECTORS THE DECISION TO COMMIT MUNICIPAL WATER, INFRASTRUCTURE, PROPERTY, OR FISCAL
RESOURCES TO SUCH FACILITIES, AND LIMITING THE LEGISLATIVE AUTHORITY OF THE TOWN BOARD TO
AUTHORIZE OR FUND SUCH FACILITIES.**

### Section 1. Findings and Declaration of Public Policy.

**WHEREAS**, the Town of Paonia is a statutory municipality organized under C.R.S. § 31-1-203,
and the registered electors of the Town have reserved to themselves the power of initiative under
Article V, Section 1(9) of the Colorado Constitution and C.R.S. § 31-11-104;

**WHEREAS**, the Town's municipal water supply, wastewater system, streets, rights-of-way, and
other public infrastructure and real property are held in trust for the use and benefit of the
Town's residents, and the water of every natural stream in Colorado is declared to be the property
of the public under Article XVI, Section 5 of the Colorado Constitution;

**WHEREAS**, the civic character of Paonia — as an agricultural community of approximately
fifteen hundred residents whose homes, farms, and orchards depend upon a finite municipal water
supply — is incompatible with the diversion of that supply and of the Town's limited
infrastructure to industrial computing facilities, which at scale may consume millions of gallons
of water per day for equipment cooling and electrical loads measured in megawatts;

**WHEREAS**, the Colorado General Assembly has repeatedly considered legislation concerning the
development and regulation of data center facilities, including Senate Bill 25-280 (Data Center
Development & Grid Modernization), House Bill 26-1030 (data center tax incentives), and Senate
Bill 26-102 (data center energy, water, and ratepayer protections), and upon the failure of the
2026 bills the regulation of data center siting and impacts has been left to Colorado's local
governments, many of which regulate data center facilities by ordinance — thereby treating data
center development as a subject appropriate to legislative action;

**WHEREAS**, this Ordinance does not concern the management or operation of any Town property
or asset; it concerns the relationship between the Town government and the people who reside in,
work in, and visit the Town of Paonia, and the disposition of resources the Town holds in trust
for them;

**WHEREAS**, the question whether a municipal government shall commit its water, infrastructure,
property, or fiscal resources to data center facilities is a question of fundamental public policy
of permanent and general character, properly the subject of municipal legislation under the
people's reserved initiative power, and not a question of administrative implementation;

**WHEREAS**, the people of the Town of Paonia are exercising the Town's police power under
C.R.S. §§ 31-15-101 and 31-15-401 to protect the health, safety, welfare, water security, and
civic character of the Town and its residents;

**NOW, THEREFORE, the people of the Town of Paonia HEREBY DECLARE** it to be the public policy
of the Town of Paonia that the commitment of the Town's municipal water, wastewater,
infrastructure, real property, or fiscal resources to data center facilities is a decision of
fundamental public policy reserved to the registered electors of the Town, and **HEREBY ORDAIN**
the following:

### Section 2. Reservation to the Electors; Limitation on Town Authority.

(a) **Policy Established.** The decision whether the Town of Paonia shall commit its municipal
water, wastewater, infrastructure, real property, or fiscal resources to any data center facility
is reserved to the registered electors of the Town. This reservation is a permanent and general
policy of the Town of Paonia within the meaning of Article V, Section 1(9) of the Colorado
Constitution.

(b) **Limitation on Town Board Authority.** It shall be unlawful for the Town of Paonia, or the
Town Board acting on its behalf, to adopt any ordinance, resolution, contract authorization,
utility service agreement, lease or conveyance of Town real property, or budget appropriation
that funds, authorizes, or otherwise enables a data center facility, unless the commitment has
first been approved by a majority of the registered electors of the Town voting on the question
at a regular or special municipal election.

### Section 3. Definitions.

For purposes of this Ordinance:

(a) **"Data center facility"** means a facility, or portion of a facility, whose primary purpose
is to house networked computer, server, or data-storage equipment providing computing,
processing, artificial-intelligence, cryptocurrency-mining, or data-storage services primarily
for users, customers, or purposes located off the premises, and that meets any of the following:
(i) a design electrical load of [500] kilowatts or more; (ii) [5,000] square feet or more of
floor area dedicated to such equipment; or (iii) water consumption for equipment cooling
exceeding [10,000] gallons per day.

(b) **"Commit"** means to sell, supply, lease, convey, dedicate, extend, appropriate, pledge, or
contract the Town's municipal water, wastewater capacity, infrastructure, real property, or
fiscal resources, in whole or in part, whether directly or through any intermediary.

(c) **"The Town"** means the Town of Paonia, Colorado, including the Town Board, all Town
departments, all elected and appointed officials, all employees, all agents, and all contractors
acting on behalf of the Town.

(d) **"Authorize"** means to enable by ordinance, resolution, contract, budget appropriation,
utility service agreement, lease, conveyance, license, permit, formal directive, or any other
exercise of Town legislative, executive, or contracting authority.

### Section 4. Exceptions.

The reservation and prohibition established in Section 2 do not apply to the following, which are
not "data center facilities" within the meaning of this Ordinance:

(a) **Municipal information systems** owned or used by the Town solely for the conduct of Town
government operations, including records, utility billing, and administrative computing;

(b) **Public safety communications equipment** used for emergency dispatch, 911 service, or the
communications of law enforcement, fire, or emergency medical agencies serving the Town;

(c) **Local telecommunications and broadband distribution equipment** — such as switching,
routing, or co-location equipment — whose primary purpose is delivering internet or
telecommunications service to residents and businesses within the Town and surrounding
communities;

(d) **On-premises computing** incidental to the operations of a business, school, library,
medical facility, or other institution located within the Town, serving that institution's own
operations and falling below the thresholds of Section 3(a).

These exceptions are an integral part of the substantive policy declared in Section 2 and define
the scope of the reservation established therein.

### Section 5. No Delegation or Indirect Commitment.

The Town Board may not authorize, contract with, fund, license, permit, or otherwise enable any
person or entity — public or private, including any enterprise, authority, special district,
urban renewal authority, or party to an intergovernmental agreement — to commit the Town's
municipal water, wastewater, infrastructure, real property, or fiscal resources to a data center
facility on the Town's behalf or under the Town's authority. This Ordinance applies with equal
force to direct Town action and to third-party action authorized, funded, or facilitated by the
Town.

### Section 6. Permanent and General Character.

This Ordinance is permanent in operation. It contains no sunset provision. It applies to all
departments, officers, employees, agents, and contractors of the Town of Paonia, in all
geographic areas subject to the Town's jurisdiction, at all times. It declares a public policy of
permanent and general character within the meaning of Article V, Section 1(9) of the Colorado
Constitution.

### Section 7. Liberal Construction.

This Ordinance shall be liberally construed to effectuate its purpose of reserving to the
electors the commitment of the Town's resources to data center facilities as established in
Section 2.

### Section 8. Severability.

If any provision of this Ordinance, or its application to any person or circumstance, is held
invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, the remaining provisions and applications shall
continue in full force and effect.

### Section 9. Effective Date.

This Ordinance takes effect upon certification of the vote.

## Parity map — v7 (Surveillance) ↔ v1 (Data Center)

This table is the seed of the two-column exhibit. Every "administrative" hook in v7 has an exact
counterpart here. Rejecting one while accepting the other (or while the Board's own data center
ordinance advances) requires articulating a distinction that does not exist.

| Element | Surveillance v7 | Data Center v1 | What differential treatment exposes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument | Initiated ordinance | Initiated ordinance | Board's own drafts on both subjects are also ordinances — the legislative instrument |
| Operative prohibition | "unlawful … to adopt any ordinance, resolution, contract authorization, or budget appropriation that funds, authorizes, or otherwise enables …" (§ 2(b)) | Identical verbs + "utility service agreement, lease or conveyance" (§ 2(b)) | If contract/appropriation language = "administrative," both are; if not, neither is |
| Touches Town infrastructure | Cameras on Town poles/buildings; Verkada contract | Town water, wastewater, land, fiscal resources | The "cameras are infrastructure maintenance" theory applies equally to water contracts — or to neither |
| "Not property management" disclaimer | WHEREAS: "does not concern the management or operation of any Town property or asset" | Verbatim same clause | Same self-description, same test |
| "Legislative subject" showing | General Assembly legislated on surveillance (SB 22-113, HB 24-1130) | General Assembly repeatedly considered data center legislation (SB 25-280, HB 26-1030, SB 26-102); siting left to local ordinance | State treats both as legislative subjects |
| Permanent & general | Explicit § 6 (Zwerdlinger language) | Verbatim § 6 | Identical |
| Narrow exceptions defining scope | 4 exceptions (§ 4) | 4 exceptions (§ 4) | Identical architecture |
| No-delegation clause | § 5 (no contractor workaround) | § 5 (no enterprise/district/IGA workaround) | Identical |
| Rights/policy core | Civic right against Town surveillance | Elector reservation over Town resource commitments | Both govern the Town-to-people relationship, not day-to-day operations |

Deliberate scope choice: land-use/zoning approvals are **excluded** from § 2(b)'s hooks.
Site-specific land-use decisions are the one genuinely quasi-judicial (administrative) zone in
this subject area — including them would hand the Clerk a colorable rejection ground. The
ordinance reaches only the Board's legislative and contracting acts, exactly as v7 did.
