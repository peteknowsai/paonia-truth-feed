# Request to the Board of Trustees

**Subject: A review of how the Town has answered public records requests**

**Date: August 4, 2026**
**From: Pete McCarthy, resident**

---

## What I am asking for

I am asking the Board to look at this.

Specifically: put one item on a work session agenda — a review of how the Town has answered public records requests since 2024.

That is the entire request. What the Board concludes from that review, and what it decides to do about it, is the Board's to decide. It is not mine to prescribe.

---

## Why

Three examples. None of them asks you to believe me about anything — no motives, no mind-reading. All three are proven by the Town's own documents, and all three come down to dates.

### 1. Impossible in 2024. Routine in 2026. By then the records were gone.

In the fall of 2024 I served on the Planning Commission. I emailed the Town Administrator a set of questions at 1:13 PM and asked that they be included in the packet for that evening's meeting. At the meeting that night he said, on camera, "Oh, we do have an email from Pete" — as though seeing it for the first time.

Microsoft 365 keeps a record of when an email is opened. I asked for it.

- **September 27, 2024** — I request the Town's Microsoft 365 audit logs.
- **September 30, 2024** — Denied. The records are "not in the Town's custody." No explanation of who did have them.
- **November 18, 2024** — I send a formal complaint to all seven trustees asking the Board to obtain the logs itself. Not one trustee responded.
- **November 25, 2024** — Jeff Roberts, Executive Director of the Colorado Freedom of Information Coalition, puts it in writing: the statute the Town cited is a notification requirement, not grounds for denial. The Town holds the license. The Town is the custodian.
- **Spring 2025** — The records age out. The Town retains these logs 180 days, the shortest tier Microsoft sells. They are permanently gone.
- **May 2026** — The Town produces **1,134,799 rows** of exactly this category of record.

Same custodian. Same email system. Same kind of record. Opposite answer.

Those logs would have shown whether the Administrator had opened that email before the meeting where he said he was seeing it for the first time. The request was denied. The records aged out. Nobody ever found out, and now nobody can.

### 2. The Town said the records did not exist. Two months later it produced them.

- **April 6, 2026** — I request the emails and drafts behind the Board's March 30 letter about me.
- **April 21, 2026** — The Town produces four emails, two of which I wrote myself, and no drafts. It certifies the response complete.
- **June 1, 2026** — I request again, naming the exact documents from the Town's own computer records.
- **June 19, 2026** — The Town produces the drafts.

The drafts existed in April. They were producible in April. They were not produced, not listed as withheld, and no reason has ever been given.

### 3. The Town erased records while my requests for them were open.

- **May 12, 2026** — I ask this Board, on camera, to preserve the Verkada camera data because it may be the subject of legal action.
- **June 4, 2026** — I request the camera system's "person of interest" and license plate lists.
- **June 8, 2026** — I request the camera activity log. Both requests are now open.
- **June 10, 2026** — The Board ends the Verkada contract. All data is erased.
- **June 11–12, 2026** — Both requests are denied with the same sentence: "no data exists to be searched."

The Town knows those lists existed, because the Town sold me the log that proves it — request C 26-09, which I paid $167.90 for.

---

## Why this matters to the Board

Paonia has been told twice, by two different outsiders.

In the Brunner case, Judge Steven Schultz of the Delta County District Court found the Town "failed to exercise reasonable diligence or reasonable inquiry" in answering a records request, and ordered the Town to pay that resident's legal costs. In 2024, CFOIC's Executive Director put in writing that the denial to me was improper.

Both times, nothing changed afterward.

Colorado law awards court costs and attorney fees to a resident who is improperly denied records and prevails in court. That bill lands on the Town's budget — the same money as streets and water. The Town has no written policy governing records requests at all; I asked for one, and the response confirmed none exists.

I'll be direct about the part that involves this body. In November 2024 I sent a formal complaint to all seven trustees asking them to obtain those logs. Not one replied. The records were still recoverable on the day that complaint was sent. They are not now.

Most of you were not on this Board then. That is exactly why I am bringing it back.

---

## What the review would look like

Not an investigation. Not an audit with a budget. A defined look at a defined stack of paper — the requests from 2024 to today. Three questions each:

1. **Was it answered inside the deadline the law sets?** Three working days, or ten with a documented extension.
2. **Was everything responsive actually produced?**
3. **Where something was withheld or called non-existent, is the written reason on file that state law requires?**

Every answer is already in a file drawer. Then a report back to the public, at a public meeting.

**One caution on who conducts it.** The review should not be run by the same people whose responses are under review. That is not an accusation — it is just how a review works. CFOIC examines these matters at no cost and has already written to this Town once. A trustee committee would also do the job.

---

## What I am not asking for

I am not telling this Board what to conclude, and I am not asking it to take an action against any employee. I have deliberately left that out.

I am asking you to look at it. If you look at these requests and reach conclusions about how this office has been run, those are your conclusions to reach and yours to act on. You have the authority. You do not need me to tell you how to use it, and it is not my place to.

What I would ask is that the looking actually happen, in public, and that whatever comes of it be said out loud.
